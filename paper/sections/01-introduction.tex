\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An organisation adopting LLM agents is usually offered three architectures: run open models
on its own hardware (private, capped by that hardware), call frontier APIs (excellent, and the
material leaves), or run its own weights in a rented cloud (a compromise that costs an MLOps
team). The industry argues about which column wins. We argue that the argument is the
mistake: \emph{the right column is a property of the request, not of the company}.
Summarising a public tender and reasoning over a client's file are different problems, and
the second does not become safe because procurement signed a data-processing agreement.

The unit of decision is therefore the \emph{step}, and something has to take that decision
consistently, thousands of times a day, and be able to show afterwards what it decided.
Three bodies of work come close, and none of them owns that decision:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Information-flow control for agents} --- CaMeL~\citep{debenedetti2025camel},
    FIDES~\citep{costa2025fides}, RTBAS~\citep{zhong2025rtbas}, Progent~\citep{shi2025progent},
    GAAP~\citep{stanley2026gaap} --- labels data and checks, at every tool call, which data
    may reach which \emph{tool or recipient}. The model that reads the data is assumed to be
    one provider; where the inference itself runs is not a policy variable.
  \item \textbf{Privacy-aware routing and local--cloud collaboration} ---
    PAPILLON~\citep{li2025papillon}, PRISM~\citep{zhan2026prism},
    Minions~\citep{narayan2025minions} --- chooses per \emph{prompt} between a local and a
    cloud model, or lets a local model write what the cloud sees. Gates are learned or soft,
    leakage is measured rather than prevented, and nothing is left for an auditor.
  \item \textbf{AI gateways} route token streams to endpoints with a fallback list ordered by
    availability. They never see a file or execute a tool, and when the preferred endpoint is
    down they fail over --- which, for sensitive material, is the leak.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{\annona.} We make the execution substrate of every agent step --- each inference
and each tool call --- the enforced object of a default-deny policy over a monotone
sensitivity class (\cref{sec:design}). Its contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Placement as the enforced object, fail-closed.} A step is placed on one of
    several trust tiers by the rule for its class; the class of a run only rises, so the
    transcript cannot be walked around by ordering steps; a step no permitted substrate can
    take is \emph{held}, never degraded. A tool that reaches the network is treated as what it
    is --- an undeclared substrate --- with a ceiling of its own. The last point closes a gap
    that any perimeter guarding inference alone has: in our own system before this work, a
    browser tool let 80.4\% of restricted documents out through its arguments
    (\cref{sec:eval-leak}).
  \item \textbf{Verifiable placement evidence.} The ledger binds every run to the fingerprint of
    its policy, and records candidates, rejections and the run's class with every decision.
    From the ledger and the policy file alone, \texttt{annona comply} re-derives each
    placement and checks six invariants (bound, sound, optimal, hold-justified, monotone,
    egress). Injected placement faults are each caught by the invariant that names them, with
    zero false positives (\cref{sec:eval-compliance}); a witness checkpoint closes truncation
    and re-hashed rewrites, the two edits a chain cannot see (\cref{sec:eval-tamper}).
  \item \textbf{A composed leak bound.} Classes from paths and patterns miss material stored
    where the policy does not expect it. A calibrated content judge raises the class on top of
    them, with a threshold fitted by split conformal prediction~\citep{vovk2005alrw} so that
    it misses a restricted text with probability at most $\alpha$. Because placement is sound
    for the class it receives and the class only rises, the leak rate is bounded by the
    misfiled share times $\alpha$ (\cref{sec:guarantees}); the measured rates sit inside the
    bound (\cref{sec:eval-leak}).
  \item \textbf{An evaluation that is not circular.} A leak rate of zero measured with canaries
    the policy lists proves only that string matching works. Our canaries are unique per
    document and listed nowhere; ground truth comes from the generator, the agent is
    adversarial, every substrate is a wiretap. We also run AgentDojo~\citep{debenedetti2024agentdojo}
    through the unmodified agent loop with a local model (\cref{sec:eval-dojo}). The harness is
    released with the system.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} None of \annona's mechanisms is new on its own: default-deny
policy over labelled data, local briefs for a cloud model, pseudonymisation with local
restoration, hash-chained logs and canaries all have precedents (\cref{sec:related}). The claim
is the unit of control --- the substrate of every step --- together with fail-closed hold
semantics and evidence a third party can check. The ledger records what the daemon decided,
not a proof of what ran; closing that gap needs attestation of the writer
(\cref{sec:discussion}).

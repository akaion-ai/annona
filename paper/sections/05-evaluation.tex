\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Every number below is produced by a script in \texttt{experiments/} and stored in
\texttt{experiments/results/}; the scripts run the production loop and perimeter unchanged.
Unless stated, runs need no network and no GPU and are deterministic from their seeds.

\paragraph{Harness.} A seeded corpus assigns a third of the documents to each class. Every
restricted document carries a canary --- a random string unique to it and \emph{listed in no
policy} --- and a fraction of them is \emph{misfiled}: saved where the policy's paths say
\pub, like an offer in a shared folder. The agent is scripted and adversarial: it reads the
document, then tries to send its full text out through the browser, then answers. Every
substrate is a wiretap and the browser is a network sink. A leak is a canary of a truly
restricted document observed by a sink whose ceiling is below \res. Ground truth comes from the
generator, never from the policy, which is what makes the rate a measurement rather than a
check that string matching works. The deployment has three substrates: on-premises GPU
(\res), EU cluster (\inte), frontier API (\pub); the local GPU is down in a quarter of the
tasks.

\Cref{tab:leak} separates the three failure modes. \emph{Failover}: the gateway leaks every
restricted document whose local GPU was down --- 98 to the EU cluster --- which \annona{}
holds instead (the 6.5\% held). \emph{Tool egress}: before the network-tool ceiling, the
browser carried the full text of 402 of 500 restricted documents off the machine; every
document the run did not hold. \emph{Classification}: with paths and patterns only, \annona{}
leaks exactly the misfiled documents (113 of 113, 22.6\%) and nothing else --- the perimeter is
sound for the class it is given, and the class is the weak point. A content judge closes most
of it: at $\alpha{=}0.05$ the leak is 0.8\%, with 3.5\% of misfiled documents leaking; at
$\alpha{=}0.01$, 0.2\% and 0.9\%. Both are within the bound of \cref{prop:leak} (misfiled share
$\times\,\alpha$: 1.1\% and 0.23\%). The cost is 1.9 points more held tasks, which are the
misfiled documents now recognised as restricted while the local GPU is down.

The judge in this experiment is simulated with a known, fixed miss rate --- it isolates the
composition, not the quality of any particular classifier. \todo{repeat with a real judge

\subsection{E3 --- Can an auditor tell a faulty perimeter from the record?}
\label{sec:eval-compliance}

We inject six faults a placement runtime could plausibly ship, run the same workload (450
tasks per fault), and audit each ledger against the \emph{approved} policy file
(\cref{tab:mutants}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X r >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
Injected fault & What it does & Leak & Violations reported \\
\midrule
none & --- & 0.167 & none (2{,}626 entries) \\
failover-widening & when nothing permitted is up, place on anything that is & 0.413 & sound 74 \\
stale-class & place on the payload's class, forgetting what the run read & 1.000 & monotone 550 \\
inverted-preference & take the rule's last choice & 0.167 & optimal 799 \\
spurious-hold & hold restricted work though a permitted substrate is up & 0.167 & hold-justified 88 \\
policy-drift & run under an edited policy, show the approved one & 0.413 & bound 1{,}800, sound 74, optimal 176 \\
egress-ceiling-off & do not enforce the network-tool ceiling & 0.753 & egress 238 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{E3: each fault is caught by the invariant that names it; the correct runtime yields no
violation. Leak is measured as in E2 (paths only, 20\% misfiled).}
\label{tab:mutants}
\end{table}

Two faults do not leak --- they cost quality (\emph{inverted-preference}) or availability
(\emph{spurious-hold}) --- and are caught anyway: the audit checks that the decision was the
prescribed one, not only that it was safe. \emph{Stale-class}, the worst fault (every
restricted document leaks), writes entries that are individually sound: each placement matches
the class it records. Only the \emph{monotone} invariant, which compares a placement with what
the run had already read, exposes it.

\paragraph{Evidence independence.} In the first version of this experiment
\emph{egress-ceiling-off} went undetected, although it leaked 75\% of restricted documents. The
same function decided both whether a tool had a ceiling and whether to label its calls
\texttt{network:}; disabling the ceiling also relabelled the calls \texttt{local}, erasing the
evidence. We now label a call by what the tool \emph{is}, and the auditor derives which tools
reach the network from the policy rather than trusting the label. The lesson generalises: the
field that records a control must not be computed by the control.

\subsection{E4 --- Tamper evidence}
\label{sec:eval-tamper}

We applied every single edit an insider could make to each entry of a real 360-entry ledger
(\cref{tab:tamper}). Field edits, byte flips and swaps are always detected and located at the
edited entry. Deletion is detected in 359 of 360 cases; the exception is the last line, which
is a truncation. Truncation and a rewrite that re-hashes every later entry are never detected
by the chain alone, as expected; with a witness holding a checkpoint every 10 entries both are
detected in every case below the last checkpoint, and with one every 50, in 97.2\% --- the
remainder being exactly the entries written after the last checkpoint.

Our design document sets the budget that decides whether a perimeter is left on: ``if the
prefect adds 200\,ms per step it will be turned off''. The perimeter adds at most 3.3\,ms per
step at the render limit and about 0.3\,ms on a typical 4k-character payload --- two to three
orders of magnitude below that, and negligible next to inference. A year of decisions at
10{,}000 steps a day (3.65 million entries) is verified and audited in about three minutes.
